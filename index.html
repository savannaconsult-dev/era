What It Does

Tool 01 is the foundation of your entire evaluation. It walks you through five requirement categories and lets you score each vendor against each requirement, weighted by importance. The result is a comparable total score per vendor that feeds the dashboard automatically.

What It Covers

CategoryFocus
Functional CapabilitiesCore accounting, project tracking, reporting, and integration features
Compliance & RegulatoryDCAA, 2 CFR 200, and other regulatory requirements (tagged by audience)

Cost & Commercial TermsPricing structure, contract terms, and total cost transparency

How to Use It

  1. Enter your organization name and the names of up to three vendors
  2. Work through each requirement category and score each vendor from 0 (does not meet) to 4 (exceeds requirements)
  3. Pay close attention to Compliance criteria — GovCon and Nonprofit items are tagged
  4. Do not skip requirements you are unsure about — score them 0 or 1 and note the gap
  5. Click Generate Scorecard Results to see ranked vendor scores by section and overall
GovCon Note
Criteria tagged as GovCon are specifically relevant to DCAA accounting system adequacy. These carry higher weights and should be scored carefully.

1. Getting Started

What is eRA?

eRA is a self-service ERP evaluation system built for small organizations making their first serious systems decision — or replacing an ERP that no longer fits. It gives you a structured, vendor-neutral process that covers every critical failure point in the selection journey, from documenting requirements through go-live readiness.

The product consists of six interactive tools organized inside a single HTML file. Each tool handles one phase of the ERP selection process. A live dashboard ties them together and updates automatically as you work.

Who Should Use eRA?

  • Government contractors selecting an ERP that needs to pass DCAA pre-award accounting system review
  • Nonprofits evaluating fund accounting systems under 2 CFR 200 federal award requirements
  • Growing businesses outgrowing QuickBooks and making their first serious ERP decision

Work through the tools in sequence (01 → 06). Tool 1 builds the vendor scorecard that feeds the dashboard. Tool 2 requires vendor names set up first. Tools 3–6 are more independent but build on earlier decisions.

2. The Dashboard

The Dashboard is your home base. It shows a live summary of results from all completed tools and updates automatically as you work through the analyzer. Open it at any time by clicking Dashboard in the sidebar.

Progress Pills

At the top of the dashboard, six progress pills show the status of each tool at a glance — Not started, In progress, or Complete. Click any pill to jump directly to that tool.

Dashboard Widgets

As you complete tools, the dashboard populates with four live widgets:

  • Requirements Scorecard — shows vendor scores by section with a winner highlight
  • 3-Year TCO — side-by-side cost bars showing total cost of ownership per vendor
  • Go-Live Readiness — your readiness score and any blocker items identified
  • Key Risk Flags — prioritized risk items surfaced across all tools

Print Summary Report

Click the Print Summary Report button at the top right of the dashboard to generate a print-ready PDF of your complete ERP evaluation. This is the deliverable you share with stakeholders or keep on file.

Best Practice
Print the summary report at the end of your evaluation before closing the browser, as your session data does not persist automatically.

6. Tips by Audience

6.1 Government Contractors

Your most critical tools are 01 (Requirements Scorecard), 03 (DCAA Readiness), and 06 (Contract Review). Focus on these before shortlisting vendors.

  • In Tool 01: pay particular attention to criteria tagged GovCon — these map directly to DCAA accounting system requirements
  • In Tool 03: complete the DCAA assessment even if you only have fixed-price contracts now. If you ever pursue cost-type vehicles, your ERP must be ready
  • In Tool 06: every CRITICAL item in the Contract Review Checklist applies to GovCon. Do not waive DCAA-related provisions in vendor contracts
  • Migration strategy (Tool 05): use Parallel Run if you have active contracts at go-live — billing continuity depends on it

6.2 Nonprofits with Federal Awards

Your most critical tools are 01 (Requirements Scorecard), 02 (TCO Calculator), and 06 (Playbook). 2 CFR 200 compliance requirements should drive your vendor evaluation.

  • In Tool 01: look for the 2 CFR 200 criteria in the Compliance & Regulatory section — these are tagged nonprofit
  • In Tool 02: nonprofit ERP implementations often underestimate internal staff costs. Be conservative with your training and project lead hour estimates
  • Tool 03 (DCAA Guide): if you only have grant awards and no FAR-based contracts, the scope check will indicate this tool does not apply
  • In Tool 06: review the Contract Review Checklist carefully around data portability — you need to be able to export your data if the vendor relationship ends

6.3 Growing Businesses Replacing QuickBooks

Your most critical tools are 01 (Requirements Scorecard), 02 (TCO Calculator), and 04 (RFI & Demo Script). The goal is to avoid being dazzled by demos and buying more than you need.

  • In Tool 01: be ruthless about requirements. Score low anything you do not actually need — vendors will upsell you on features you will never use
  • In Tool 02: the hidden internal cost calculation in Tool 02 is especially important for small teams. A 300-hour project lead commitment is real capacity you are pulling from the business
  • Tool 04 (Demo Script): use it exactly as written. Vendors routinely show demos in an order designed to impress rather than compare. The script neutralizes this
  • Tool 05 (Data Migration): if you have 5+ years of QuickBooks data, your migration complexity is higher than you think. Complete this audit before signing
